# Mochitsuki: CLAUDE.md

An immersive three.js scene of mochi pounding (mochitsuki). Since 2026-11-15 there are two worlds, picked in the start menu's Worlds panel (`src/worlds.js`): the Moon garden (the default since 2026-11-15, the user's pick) and the snowy shrine courtyard on New Year's Eve, ported from the Unity team's environment. Both are `ready` (the Moon garden since 2026-11-15; it loads without errors but has not been seen on the headset). It runs on desktop (pointer lock + WASD) and in WebXR (Quest 3), and Satie provides the spatial sound.

## Layout

- `src/layout.js`: every position in metres (three.js frame, +Y up, the camera starts facing −Z toward the mortar). Both the meshes and the Satie manifest read these numbers.
- `src/worlds.js` (2026-11-15, the user's Worlds menu): the registry of worlds. Each entry has `id`, `name`, `blurb`, `ready`, and `build(scene, renderer, { steam })`.
  - **What `build` returns:** `colliders` (with an optional `rect` clamp), `update(dt, elapsed, head)`, optional `setProgress(p)`, `spawn`, `enterLabel`, and `sound { hosts, muted, mixGroups }`. main.js sets `onSound` on the result (the shrine's bell calls it).
  - **No heavy imports:** each world's code loads by a dynamic import inside its `build`. A broken or unfinished world therefore never stops the page or the menu; main.js falls back to the shrine courtyard (or, if the courtyard is what failed, to `DEFAULT_WORLD`, which is 'moon' since 2026-11-15, the user's pick) and says so in `#world-note`.
  - **`?world=<id>`** opens any listed world, ready or not. menu.js owns the hidden `#world` select and reloads with the param. A world that isn't `ready` shows as a greyed "Coming soon" card.
  - **main.js** builds the world before `createStation` (kineContact reads the 'ground' mesh once). It then teleports the player to `world.spawn` (player.js starts at `L.SPAWN`). Satie fixtures come from `world.sound.hosts`; any a world lacks, plus the pounder and helper, are bound to empty markers. Sources in `world.sound.muted` get statement volume 0.
  - **The shrine entry:** `buildWorld` from src/world.js, plus snow, the bonfire's embers and the steamer's steam (src/effects.js). It uses the courtyard's rect clamp and spawns at [0, 0, 10.5] facing the mortar, with the full soundscape.
  - **The moon entry:** builds the Moon garden and its guarded story layer. `sound.createAmbience` now supplies a local ambient layer, independent of the shrine recordings.
- `src/moonGarden.js` + `src/moonGarden/` (2026-11-15): the Moon garden, ported from the Unity team's environment.
  - **Source:** MochiPound branch `multiplayer`, `Assets/_Project/World/`: EnvironmentBuilder.cs, MeshBuilder, the MR_EnvironmentSettings values, GardenProps, SkyMist, the Celestial/Glow shaders, and MoonStory's progress visuals.
  - **What it is:** a moonlit clearing on a floating island, ringed by about 300 instanced bamboo stalks, with an opening toward -Z for the Moon and the Earth. It has:
    - the cassia tree, two stone lanterns, stepping stones, floating rocks and sky mist;
    - tzhu235's props, loaded asynchronously from `public/env/` (paper lanterns, wind chime, bench, Moon Rabbit sign, bamboo fountain, mossy stones, fireflies).
  - **Coordinates:** it is built in Unity's coordinates under a root scaled (1, 1, -1), so the Unity +Z opening is three's -Z. The props sit under a further (-1, 1, 1), as Unity mirrors imported OBJs.
  - **Random layout:** Unity's System.Random is ported exactly, and so is its Perlin noise (checked against a height baked into the Unity prefab).
  - **Look:** Unity's Built-in pipeline in linear colour, with no post-processing. So buildMoonGarden sets `renderer.toneMapping = NoToneMapping` at exposure 1, which also changes how the station looks (`{ keepToneMapping: true }` keeps ACES).
  - **`setProgress(p)`** (eased; main.js feeds it the batch progress, itself eased):
    - the Moon waxes from a crescent to full and the night brightens;
    - paper lanterns light from 12%, stone lanterns from 25% and the tree's glow from 20%; blossoms from 50%, stepping stones from 75%, and the bamboo turns silver from 90%.
  - **Colliders:** about 405 circles, including a ring at the island's edge. There are no rectangle bounds: player.js clamps only to a world's `colliders.rect`, which the courtyard has and the garden doesn't.
  - **Story (`src/moonGarden/story/`, continued on `3js`):** the four Chuseok lines rise onto a translucent hanji scroll, followed by an angular portal from a starry void into the garden. Desktop Space/Enter/E and VR select or a 0.35 s held pinch skip. Intro input precedes song-scroll input; strikes and song playback wait for the reveal. Ending reset restarts the intro and yields the Earth's light to it. `text.js` uses the bundled Cinzel/Baloo fonts and shared atlases; `scroll.js` draws the paper/rolls/cloud corners. Literal guarded imports include all parts in production bundles.
  - **Song scrolls:** one per listed song, outside the 1.6 m kneading zone. E while looking within 2.5 m, or controller select within 0.5 m, equips the song's mallet and selects its music. Bangarang = Tteokme, Believer = Yokogine, Gangnam = Jeolgut-gongi. `mallets.js` builds direct-child cylinders/tori so the station's collision code reads their real dimensions; the pestle has its own vertical swing, rest and VR grip. Music fades out at batch completion; the next intro precedes another choice. Batch progression still uses the three.js station's strike counter.
  - **Cost and status (estimate, not measured):**
    - about 66 draw calls and 276k triangles per view; the props are 152k of the triangles, and the paper-lantern stand alone is 56k. The fountain's OBJ (1.7 MB) parses at load.
    - Not yet looked at on the headset.

Sound: in the garden, shrine-specific loops and footsteps remain muted (`sound.muted` in src/worlds.js); the garden has its own wind/water/chimes (`src/moonGarden/ambience.js`). Missing shrine fixtures are bound to markers. The shrine courtyard plays its full original soundscape.
  - Some of the older notes below still describe the NPCs. Details:
  - A palm entering the drawn dough at 0.3 m/s or more emits `turn`, and a hand in the bucket water emits `dip` and wets the dough.
  - `handsIn` means a tracked joint is over the mortar, within the rim plate's radius and up to one head height above it. While it is true, the NPC pounder holds at the top, and in rhythm mode it lets the note go. An NPC swing that lands on a hand doesn't count. A player strike with hands in is "rushed".

- `src/hands.js` (2026-11-14): WebXR hand tracking (`hand-tracking` is requested by VRButton in main.js). Since 2026-11-15 the hands are drawn from their tracked joints: instanced spheres per joint and tubes per bone, at the reported radii, the same shapes the dough and mallet feel. three's generic hand mesh, loaded from a CDN with no error handling, left a tracked hand invisible for the session when its download dropped. It could also land on the wrong hand when the headset reordered its inputs. `[hands] <side> hand tracked` and `... lost by the headset` are logged to .tmp/dev.log, to tell the headset losing a hand apart from a drawing problem. Colliders come from the tracked joints only. For the dough, every bone is a capped cylinder of its joints' mean radius, 24 per hand, with `group` set to the hand so the dough can skip a whole hand with one bounding check. One body (2026-11-15: the user never wants the dough to come apart into blobs, only stretch and fold).
  - **The problem:** MPM particles hold together only through shared grid nodes. Dough stretched thinner than a cell on the headset's 4.5 cm grid came apart for good. A thin strand also vanished from the drawing, because the surface sits at density 0.5.
  - **Links:** mpm-core links particles closer than `linkReach` 1.5 x their lattice spacing. Once per frame, `relink()` adds links for pairs that came within reach, so folds stick. A stretched link is dropped only if its particles are still joined through short links within `linkDepth` 8 hops. Otherwise it is a tether: every substep it is held at the reach, and its ends can't move apart along it.
  - **Drawing:** in writeField, sparse particles (density under `thinDensity` 0.42) are splatted up to `thinBoost` 4x. Links with a sparse end get extra samples every half lattice spacing, so strands are drawn as tubes.
  - **test-dough.mjs (user-approved run):** particles and the drawn surface stay one piece through the drop, strikes, a fold, fast VR swings and hard hits on tiny and full. Before, VR swings left 2-3 pieces. The one exception is one moment of a slow-motion swing on full: 2 particle pieces, still drawn as 1.
  - **Cost:** about +25-30% per frame (tiny 2.0 vs 1.6 ms, full 15.6 vs 12 ms on juno2, which was loaded at the time).
Hands and the dough (`HAND` in hands.js): the dough is an elastic blob that sticks only to itself (the user's call, 2026-11-15). Hands are `{ tack 0, friction 1, mould 0 }`. Friction: a particle touching a hand at the start of a step (`stickSlack`) and pressed against it moves with the hand, by friction x (1 - wet). A particle the hand moves away from is released at once, so nothing follows a lifting palm. Wet hands slide. This acts on particles directly, because on the 4.5 cm headset grid each particle averages 27 nodes and node-level contact was diluted to about 20-40%. Hand bones are grouped (one bounding sphere per hand).
  - The day before, hands were sticky and moulding clay (tack 3, mould 1). The user found that far too sticky. That code is still there: tack > 0 gives a hand a hold, pressure-based on whether its contact particles push into it or away, plus a falloff follow within `pullReach`. mould > 0 drops the yield to `mouldYield` around a touching hand, so its dents stay.
  - All tools, the mallet included, are rp thicker to the dough (as the wood is), so the drawn dough meets a finger instead of swallowing it.
  - The worker's start-up probe (`pickTier`) calls `substep` without `step`, so anything `setTools` builds needs a default in the constructor. A missing `groups` default crashed the worker and left no dough (2026-11-15); test-dough.mjs now runs the probe first.
  - `scripts/test-hands.mjs [tier]` tests a synthetic hand: press, drag, two-hand squeeze-and-lift, wet press. Env knobs: `HAND_TACK`, `HAND_FRICTION`, `HAND_MOULD`, `CORE_OPTS`, and `BEFORE_CORE` to compare an older core.
  - Results on tiny with friction hands (user-approved runs):
    - Press 4 cm then lift: the dough springs back 2.2 cm with the palm and ends 1.3 cm down; nothing sticks.
    - A 10 cm drag carries the dough 6.6 cm (3.7 without friction) and it settles back to 3.7 cm.
    - A squeeze-lift barely lifts anything (1%).
    - One piece throughout.
  - Cost: frames with both hands in the dough take about 2.0 ms on one juno2 core on tiny, against about 1.5 ms without (full: 14 vs 12). A tool that reappears carries `reset`, so dough.js doesn't sweep it from its last pose. For the mallet, every joint is a sphere `{ center, r }`, and kineContact accepts these as well as sphere meshes. Whenever hands appear in XR after at least 1 s without them, and the wearer isn't holding the kine or already standing there, main.js moves the head to `KNEAD_SPOT`, facing the mortar. That's the west rim, 0.6 m from the axis; from the old kneeling spot, 0.78 m, a standing person couldn't reach the dough. The heading comes from the head's yaw, not its gaze, which is near vertical when looking down at the hands. In XR, player.js now applies collisions only to thumbstick moves, so a real person can lean over the mortar. A hand's pinch no longer counts as a controller press. The headset tracks only its wearer's hands: someone else's hands in front of the cameras are not supported. Two people (one pounding, one kneading) need two headsets and a network link, which is not built yet. None of this has been tested on the headset.
- `src/dough/`: the simulated dough. `mpm-core.js` is MLS-MPM ported from VR-Doh (`/juno/u/paruwel/immerse/MochiPound`, kernels in `scripts/mpm3D_gaussian_part.kernel.py`, Apache-2.0); every constant is in `DEFAULTS`, with VR-Doh's value noted. `mpm.worker.js` runs it off the main thread; `dough.js` draws it with three's MarchingCubes and feeds it the mallet head and the turner's hands as colliders. `scripts/test-dough.mjs` is a headless smoke test and `scripts/profile-dough.mjs` a timing breakdown (ask before running either: they are compute runs). Material (reworked 2026-11-14 after the user found the dough too runny and breaking into pieces): always one cohesive dough. Neo-Hookean, E 4 kPa (shear ~1.4 kPa; measured fresh mochi: 0.3-3 kPa), nu 0.4 (nearly incompressible), with Herschel-Bulkley viscoplasticity: elastic below the yield stress (`yieldStress` 800 Pa just-steamed -> 550 Pa kneaded, by each particle's cohesion), above it flowing at ((excess)/`flowK` 300)^(1/`flowN` 0.3), so its own weight only lets it creep while a strike flows at once. The old loose-grain phase (Drucker-Prager, no tension) is gone: it crumbled the dough. Cohesion (`work`/`workToDough`, floor rising with batch progress) now only sets the yield stress, the tack and the look. Tack (the Unity team's recipe, not physical): a tool with `tack` 1 (the mallet; the turner's wet hands are 0) pressed into the dough bonds to it; while it pulls away, dough within `pullReach` 8 cm follows with a linear falloff (a tent), and after `tackTime` 0.15 s of pulling the bond lets go (less tack on fresh rice, none when wet). Stray specks (local density under 30% of a full cell) are braked toward the lump's motion and drawn back to its centre. Tiers: 4 particles per cell on all but the headset tier (2 per cell broke flattened dough into blobs): full 2.5 cm ~2,940 particles, medium 2.8 cm, light 3.1 cm, lightest 3.5 cm ~1,070, minimal 4 cm ~720, tiny 4.5 cm at 3 per cell ~380. Steps are 0.4 cell per elastic wave crossing (3-5 substeps per 60 Hz frame); the Quest browser runs `tiny` (it measured 12-14 us per particle-substep at load), and fewer substeps there are meant to keep it out of slow motion. `?dough=<tier>` in the URL forces a tier and turns off the automatic pick. The start screen has a menu for it (2026-11-15, main.js). "Dough: automatic" is the default; picking a tier reloads the page with `?dough=<tier>`, and a note beside it shows the tier that is running. Speed limits (Unity team's values): a tool hands the dough at most `maxToolSpeed` 5 m/s, and no particle moves more than `maxDoughSpeed` 2.5 m/s relative to the lump. Stray specks: density < 0.35 of a full cell, drag 30/s, pull 6 m/s^2 back to the lump centre (2 left specks torn off by VR swings lying around). The worker picks a tier from a timing probe (`pickTier`, 11 ms budget per frame) and re-picks at every `clear()` from the cost measured in play (up needs a 30% margin). The user's laptop browser measured ~2.75 us per particle-substep in play, a Quest browser 7.66 us in the probe (juno2: ~0.5). `scripts/test-dough.mjs` reports slump over 6 s of rest and the number of separate pieces (2026-11-14). Smoke test after the rework (2026-11-14, user-approved): on both minimal (704 particles) and full (2,928) the dough stays one piece through drop, 3 strikes and a fold, and does not slump at all over 6 s of rest (a mound ~13-16 cm above the bowl bottom). The first strike leaves a 5-7 cm dent that stays, and dry strikes tent the dough 2-3 cm above its resting top (wet: none). The mallet drags the dough under its face up 5-9 cm dry and under 1 cm wet. Cost per 60 Hz frame on one juno2 core, including the test's own bookkeeping: 2.6 ms minimal, 12.1 ms full. A step never covers more than `maxSubsteps * maxDt`. Mortar and dough (2026-11-14, after the user saw the mochi go into the bowl): the dough's mortar is the exact drawn one (`usuShape`, not the old approximate ellipsoid `usuSdfAt`, now removed). Each particle stands for a cube of side `2 rp` (`rp` = half the cube root of its volume), and marching cubes draws the surface along the outside of those cubes, so particle centres are kept `rp` from the wood (push-out in G2P and the grid's wood boundary both at `rp`). Before, centres rested on the wood and the bottom `rp` of the dough (1.6 cm on `tiny`) was drawn inside the wood. A mallet squashing dough onto the bowl floor also packed it 2-5x denser than rest while J still read ~1 (push-outs move particles without updating F). Measured density above `crowdStart` 1.15 now pushes back (the Unity team's crowdStiffness curve, at 2 kPa scaled to this step's stability limit). `.tmp/compare-wood.mjs` ran old vs new on the same drop + 3 strikes onto the wood (user-approved, 2026-11-14). The share of the dough drawn above the wood (the rest is hidden inside it): tiny 69% -> 82% at rest and 52% -> 78% after strikes; full 82% -> 90% and 61% -> 74%; minimal about the same (82/76 -> 83/74). test-dough.mjs now prints "in wood" (how far the drawn surface reaches into the wood): 0.0-0.1 cm on tiny and 0.7-0.8 cm on full through rest, strikes and a fold, with no particle cube in the wood. Up to 2-4 cm while the head sits on the wood, hidden under it. Behaviour is otherwise unchanged: one piece, no slump, dry drag 5-7 cm, wet under 1 cm. Dev-server console forwarding (vite.config.js `forwardConsole`) puts the browser's `[dough]` info lines in `.tmp/dev.log`.

- `src/props.js`: the mortar and mallet meshes, built in one place. station.js shows them, and both the collision and the dough's colliders read their sizes back from the meshes (no second copy of any dimension). `usuShape(usu)` reads the mortar's exact shape back from its meshes as plain data (cross-section, rim plate, bands, world matrix).
- `src/usuShape.js`: `usuSdf(shape, x, y, z, n)`, the mortar's exact signed distance and normal, in plain JS so the mallet's collision and the dough's worker share one function on one shape. Points within float round-off of the axis (`cos(pi/2)` = 6e-17) are snapped onto it; before, the axis counted as a surface and the distance inside the wood near the bowl's centre was wrong.
- `src/kineContact.js` (2026-11-14; the user asked for exact collision with no assumed numbers): exact collision of the mallet with the mortar, the ground and the turner's hands, read from the meshes. The mortar is an exact solid of revolution: the LatheGeometry's cross-section, the rim RingGeometry as a plate, the iron bands as exact tori, the mat and the `ground` mesh. Its signed distance is the 2D distance in the cross-section, which is exact for a solid of revolution. The mallet's head, bands and tapered handle use exact capped-cone and torus distances. Contact: the deepest point of the mallet's true surface inside the wood or ground (a scan of each patch, then gradient ascent), the cross-section's convex corners (circles) inside the mallet, and the hand spheres. All current contacts are cleared together by one pivot about the grip (or a push along the handle when that can't help), bisected to the smallest move that clears them. The mallet rests touching (test: overlap and gap both 0.000-0.001 mm). Motion marches toward the frame's pose by the free space around it (at least the handle's radius near something) and stops once blocked. Deepest points are remembered between frames. Cost: ~1.1 ms per frame in contact and 0.04 ms in the air on one juno2 core (expect ~5x on the Quest). A `kine_contact` event buzzes the controller (main.js). There's no knock sound (that needs a new Satie voice, a paid realize). `scripts/test-kine-contact.mjs` checks overlap and gap against dense samples of the true surfaces and every mortar vertex, plus the time per frame. The dough simulation uses the same exact mortar (2026-11-14). After moving to the shared shape the test still passes (overlap and gap 0.000 mm), at 0.29 ms per frame in contact.
- `src/rhythm/`: `beats.js` finds tempo and beats; `music.js` plays audio with output-latency compensation and calibration; `rhythm.js` draws and judges this headset's notes. Since 2026-11-15 the role follows the kine (`roleNow`): strike notes while this headset holds it (desktop/vr), kneading notes halfway between strikes while the other headset holds it (remote), and otherwise the default (`kneading`: the guest). It re-picks the role every frame and on each action, clearing the notes when the role changes. Rings and popups: perfect = green "Good", good = yellow "Average", miss = red (`JUDGMENT_COLORS`). Entering unlocks audio; selecting a scroll starts or changes the shared song. `scripts/test-beats.mjs` checks beat detection; `scripts/test-music-sync.mjs` checks the shared timeline and guest song requests.
- `src/net.js`, `src/sync.js` (2026-11-15): two headsets in one scene. The start-screen menu, kept as `?mp=host|join`, picks the role.
  - **Relay:** `multiplayerRelay` in vite.config.js passes messages between the pages connected to the dev server, over Vite's own WebSocket. No new server or package, dev server only. Each Quest reaches it as http://localhost:5174 through `adb reverse`, over USB or adb over Wi-Fi.
  - **Minimal sharing** (the user's choice, 2026-11-15, after streaming the host's state still lagged). Each headset runs everything itself: the batch, the NPCs and their strikes, the steamer, its own dough, the sounds, the strike count. Nothing on it waits for the network. What crosses the network (src/sync.js header):
    - **'pose', both ways, about 45 times a second:** head, tracked hand joints, and the kine while held. Each headset draws the other's head (`createAvatar`) and hands (hand slots 2 and 3), eased toward each update so they glide. Those hands are colliders in its own dough.
    - **A kine held on the other headset** puts the local station in mode `'remote'`. The NPC steps aside and the kine is eased toward the received pose. It still pounds the local dough, and `swingStrike(dt, 'remote')` counts and sounds its strikes locally.
    - **'phase' from the host,** on change and once a second. The guest (`station.setFollower(true)`) plays each phase locally and waits at a phase's natural end until the host has moved on (`followPhase`). If it is more than 1.5 s behind or out of step, `jumpTo` catches it up and tidies up: box restored, rice spawned, dough cleared. Pounding ends when the host's batch does, whatever the guest counted.
    - **Holding the kine** (either role on either headset, 2026-11-15): the guest's grip at the mortar in VR, or E on desktop (`wantKine = { desktop: true }`), sends `kine-take`. A grant arriving after the 1.5 s ask timeout, or after switching between desktop and VR, is given straight back with `kine-drop`. Entering VR while holding it on desktop drops it. A second kneader whose hands appear while the other head is within 0.45 m of `KNEAD_SPOT` goes to `KNEAD_SPOT_FAR` (north rim) instead; `sync.otherHead` is the other avatar's head. The guest's The host answers `kine-grant {ok}`; if ok, the guest holds it in local VR mode (strikes felt and counted at once) and its pose carries the kine to the host. Grabbing again sends `kine-drop`. If the guest goes quiet for 1 s, the host sends `kine-revoked`. Neither side can grab while the other holds it.
    - **One dough, the host's** (2026-11-15, after the user found the two independent doughs too different). About 12 times a second the host sends its particles (`DoughSim.snapshot`: 16-bit positions across the box and velocities within ±8 m/s, about 6 KB on tiny) as 'dough'.
      - The guest keeps simulating its own copy, so its hands act at once, and eases it toward the host's by 0.35 per update (`DoughSim.correct`).
      - It takes the host's tier (`dough.applySnapshot` locks it), so particle i matches; both spawn identically. A different count, for example after joining mid-batch, replaces the guest's particles. "No dough" snapshots are ignored, since the guest clears its own at its done phase.
      - The guest skips its local rice spawn if the host's dough already arrived.
      - The snapshots are a network delay old, so the guest's freshest dents get pulled back slightly until the host has them.
    - **Music:** `src/musicSync.js` shares the host's song, beat analysis, strike pace and playback clock. Either player can select a song scroll with E, controller select or a local tracked-hand pinch. Guest `music-pick` requests are validated by the host and acknowledged; loading requests are serialized. `ctx.canChooseSong` allows both players to use scrolls; `ctx.musicAuthority` restricts playback lifecycle control to host/solo. Scrolls stay open while loading and playing. Guest kneading notes fall halfway between host strikes. Host heartbeat and transport continue without XR render frames or host audio availability. Both players must enter to unlock audio.
    - **Shared assets:** the mallet catalog in `story/mallets.js` uses stable IDs (`yokogine`, `tteokme`, `pestle`). `station.setKineAsset(id)` caches matching local meshes and rebuilds grip/collider data. The host sends `kine-asset` on changes and periodic updates, plus `assetId` in pose packets and targeted ownership grants. Guests apply the host's asset before posing or holding it. The host accepts movement/drop only from the granted holder, and discards poses tagged with an old asset ID.
